# Writing for university visitors

English is the default edition. Write Japanese and Traditional Chinese as
natural prose in their own right; matching meaning does not require matching
sentence structure. All public changes ship in the three corresponding PO
catalogs together.

## Start with the visitor's task

A heading should tell a visitor where they are. The next sentence should help
them act. Use familiar nouns for controls: applications, sources, replies,
records, courses. Save archival metaphors for the places where they help set a
scene. Avoid giving every control its own metaphor.

| Before | After | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| This is not a personality test. | Describe your housing needs. | Gives the next action. |
| This calendar is not a static poster. | Check term dates, moon phases, and events. | Explains what the page offers. |
| Edition-two reply saved. | Reply saved. | Names the saved item. |
| Nothing you did not do gets invented. | Shows related events recorded in My TU. | Describes the actual source. |

Cut a sentence if it repeats the heading or explains an objection the visitor
has not raised. Avoid claims about how real, authentic, meaningful or unusual
the site is. Concrete details do that work: a borrowed book, a closed gate,
a duty roster, a disputed invoice.

## Preserve the information that changes a decision

Keep entry requirements, deadlines, quotas, costs, stopping conditions, consent,
withdrawal, record scope and data-loss consequences explicit. A necessary limit
is useful information. An examination's logical distinction is part of its
reasoning, as are a deliberately flawed proposal and a plausible distractor.
Do not remove those merely because they contain a negative sentence.

Explain storage once where it matters. State that records belong to this
browser and site; provide export/import instructions where visitors move them.
Keep destructive confirmations direct and specific. Loading, error and empty
states should explain status or recovery before adding a joke.

## Give characters their own interests

- Reimu wants the problem stopped, the cleanup assigned, and the bill paid.
- Marisa wants to try the experiment. Her methods can be excellent while her
  materials list remains inconvenient.
- Patchouli asks for definitions, conditions and the return date.
- Aya hears a headline. Sources, rivals and corrections interfere with it.
- Yukari can be courteous while moving the premise of the question.
- Nitori notices mechanisms, maintenance clearance and the repair price.
- Eirin is precise about dose, exposure and individual differences.
- Keine asks who recorded an event and when; the village bears consequences.
- Akyuu keeps sources, dates and objections. She need not narrate the data model.
- Faith faculty retain competing goals for access, resources and public life.

Use short, situated speech. Avoid giving every character the same lesson about
consent, evidence, cooperation and responsibility in the same three clauses.
A dispute can remain unresolved without the narrator explaining that this is
what makes the university distinctive.

## Read it in context

Read a page from its entry point through the action, result and return path.
Check Japanese particles and register, Chinese phrasing and script, English
collocations and label consistency. Read neighbouring paragraphs together:
repetition often appears between individually acceptable messages.

Then check the actual desktop and mobile layout in all three languages. A
shorter English string may require a different Japanese or Chinese line break.
PO checks protect contexts and placeholders; they cannot judge naturalness.
