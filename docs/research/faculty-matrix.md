## Public roster scope

The illustrated card grid keeps eight primary chairs for a readable overview.
The School of Faith and Coexistence Policy now has a separate rotating council:
Byakuren, Kanako, Miko and Sanae are individually explorable, with Eiki serving
as an external appeals examiner. This format makes the competing religious and
political positions visible without forcing five more cards into the main grid.
The site's groupings are pedagogical and institutional design choices, not
claims about official affiliation.

## Character–institution friction

University roles must not make the cast uniformly compliant, therapeutic or
administratively exemplary. Institutional values can remain sincere, but the
characters should regularly test, bend, exploit or misunderstand them in ways
consistent with their motives and abilities.

- Aya can teach correction while producing the headline that needs it.
- Marisa can reproduce an experiment while refusing a satisfactory account of
  where the books and materials came from.
- Yukari can expose a rule's hidden premise by moving the room, border or
  definition to which the rule applies.
- Reimu can treat a campus donation box as inseparable from keeping the
  Hakurei Shrine standing.
- Nitori can build a useful machine whose “temporary” tape repair becomes part
  of the structure and then part of the sales argument.
- Patchouli can turn an overdue-loan dispute into an argument about whose
  lifespan defines “permanent.”
- Faith faculty should disagree over resources, representation, public ritual
  and whether institutional coexistence serves living communities or merely
  produces a tidy brochure.

The design rule is productive abrasion: competence stays recognisable, but no
one becomes a generic ideal professor simply because the setting is a
university.
