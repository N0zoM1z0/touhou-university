# Spell-card Design and Ethics Workshop Notes

Research/design pass: 2026-07-26
Public implementation: `src/data/spellcard-workshop.js`,
`src/js/spellcard-workshop-model.js`, `src/js/spellcard-workshop.js`

## Editorial purpose

The workshop turns the existing spell-card readability study into something a
visitor can actually operate. It is not a universal moral calculator. A design
may be expressive but unreproducible, technically stable but exhausting, or
safe for a participant while consuming the sound and time of everyone sharing
the venue. The interface therefore keeps six review positions separate and
preserves dissent after a public ruling.

## Character anchors

The writing reuses the project’s prior local character dossiers under
`/home/pentester/Project/gensokyo-monochrome-heart/design/04_characters/`.
No dialogue or official asset is copied.

- Reimu is direct, practical and concerned with whether a declared rule and an
  actual exit remain the same thing once play begins.
- Marisa values a recognisable high-output expression, but also the apparatus,
  seed, material provenance and failed attempts needed to reproduce it.
- Aya reads the performance through an audience and a headline; her ability to
  clarify a public event remains in tension with her ability to publish the
  convenient version first.
- Nitori values mechanisms, collision stability and practical performance,
  while leaving room for taped wires, commercial shortcuts and a frame-rate
  problem that becomes part of the pattern.
- Eirin distinguishes warning from mitigation, one run from repeated exposure,
  and preserving an interesting design from putting participants inside it.
- The Misty Lake Fairy Chorus represents shared space, rhythm and play.
  Cirno’s ability to discover a route that expert reviewers missed is treated
  as a useful observation, not a competence joke.

## Workshop model

The sandbox exposes speed, density, symmetry, randomness, declaration delay,
safe-corridor width, change frequency, duration, flash level, visual cue, sound
cue and venue. Five formula-driven pattern skeletons provide different motion
families without introducing a physics engine. The renderer is capped at 72
simultaneous shots, approximately 30 frames per second and 1.5 device pixel
ratio, and it pauses outside the viewport.

The derived measurements are deliberately plural:

- effective corridor;
- projectile budget and collision workload;
- readable signal;
- expressive silhouette;
- reproducibility conditions;
- stimulation and fatigue.

No weighted total is computed. The three-seat public defence always includes
Reimu and Marisa plus the strongest unresolved external objection. It asks one
question about rules/exits, one about reproduction, and one about the external
objection. Rulings retain conditions and a named dissent rather than replacing
the six workshop notes.

## Persistence and public reactions

The visitor’s current draft, sealed design versions and defence records stay in
`tu:spellcards:draft`, `tu:spellcards:designs` and
`tu:spellcards:defences`. Saving and defending create deduplicated campus-ledger
events. BBS reactions are derived at display time: Aya’s first report, the
panel’s record and the fairy chorus’s rehearsal response all link back to the
same defence without becoming visitor-authored posts.

## Asset and rights note

The workshop adds no image asset. Its paper, tape, dais, stamps, controls and
flight court are original HTML/CSS/Canvas work. It uses no official sprites,
screenshots, spell-card images, scripts, music, scans or another fan creator’s
art.
