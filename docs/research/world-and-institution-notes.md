# World and Institution Notes

## 1. Why a university is a plausible AU translation

Gensokyo is not canonically organised as a modern university, but it already
contains multiple knowledge-producing institutions and practices:

- the Hieda family chronicles people, youkai and places;
- Keine teaches and protects the Human Village while working directly with
  history;
- Patchouli's library represents deep, specialised magical study;
- Marisa experiments, observes, catalogues and learns through repetition;
- Eientei produces and distributes medicine to humans and youkai;
- kappa design devices and work within mountain infrastructure;
- Aya and Hatate gather, frame and publish information;
- religious leaders publicly debate social order and coexistence;
- Reimu resolves incidents and maintains a boundary-facing shrine role;
- the spell-card system provides a stylised, rule-bound method of conflict.

The AU university combines these without pretending their cooperation would be
effortless. The key design idea is **“turn incidents into questions before they
become disasters.”**

## 2. Canon-grounded world anchors

### Great Hakurei Barrier

The barrier separates Gensokyo from the Outside World and is commonly described
as a boundary of common sense. The Hakurei Shrine sits at the border. The site
uses this as the conceptual basis for “Boundary and Incident Studies,” but the
faculty, curriculum and campus gate are AU inventions.

### Human Village and records

The Human Village is a centre of human life and education. Keine is explicitly
associated with teaching and guarding it; Akyuu's chronicle is written for a
human readership and includes her own limits and speculation. The site turns
this into a history/records school and teaches source criticism rather than
treating Akyuu as omniscient.

### Magic as study and practice

Patchouli represents long-form elemental and textual knowledge; Marisa
represents practical experimentation, collecting and trained effort; Alice
represents controlled doll magic and craftsmanship. Their differences make a
faculty more convincing than a single generic “magic class.”

### Eientei and medicine

Eirin is a master pharmacist/medicine figure whose work is distributed to both
humans and youkai. Eientei is hidden in the Bamboo Forest of the Lost and is
linked to lunar knowledge. “Lunar Medicine and Life Science” is therefore a
strong AU translation, with strict consent and safety language added by the
project.

### Youkai Mountain engineering

Nitori is a kappa engineer/businessperson; mountain society also includes tengu
information networks and the Moriya Shrine's interest in faith, energy and
infrastructure. The site treats the mountain as a field campus, not as a neutral
or politically unified space.

### Media and institutional bias

Aya's publication is energetic and influential, but it is an in-world source
with framing incentives. The university's media school foregrounds consent,
correction and distinguishing observation from interpretation.

### Religious pluralism

*Symposium of Post-mysticism* provides a direct model of Kanako, Byakuren and
Miko presenting competing approaches in a structured discussion. The AU turns
this into a required public seminar. It does not erase their rivalry.

## 3. AU institutional decisions

Everything in this section is project-original:

- name: 幻想鄉立東方大學 / Touhou University;
- motto: “越境求真・以禮交鋒”;
- seven schools, eighteen field stations, 108 “registered species” and all
  numerical performance claims;
- admissions routes and dates;
- all research-project titles and metrics;
- the compact between Reimu, Yukari and the Village archives;
- a campus spread across Gensokyo linked by boundary-safe paths;
- student clubs, residence system, open day and financial aid.

The website presents these with an “AU” badge in the relevant sections and an
unofficial-fan-work notice in both the header and footer.

## 4. Tone and portrayal rules

- Institutional prestige is sincere; the absurdity is in Gensokyo's subject
  matter, not in making the cast incompetent.
- Reimu is practical and responsible, not merely lazy or donation-obsessed.
- Marisa's expertise comes from work and experiment, not “stealing books.”
- Yukari is strategically indirect, not omniscient.
- Patchouli is a specialist, not a generic librarian.
- Eirin's authority does not remove consent or research ethics.
- Nitori's commercial instincts do not erase engineering competence.
- Aya's journalism is biased but not uniformly fabricated.
- Byakuren's compassion includes firm institutional boundaries.
- Miko's leadership and desire-reading do not equal consensus or mind control.
- No relationship is framed as canon romance.
