EX-L022. Which interpretation survives clock correction without exceeding the data?12 marks

Source dossier · independent conditions
[Testimony] Reisen: “I closed the moonlight gate after the second bell.” Tewi: “The gate dimmed first, then the second bell sounded.”
[Devices] Bell recorder clock is 18 seconds slow; gate controller resets to 00:00 after each full-moon switch; corridor light meter is correctly timed.
[Illuminance] 80→12 at 21:14:42; recorder labels the second bell 21:14:31.
  1. After correction the bell is about 21:14:49 and dimming came first; therefore Tewi's account is true and Reisen falsely reported the closing time
  2. Adding 18 seconds places bell two near 21:14:49, after the light drop; Tewi's observed order is compatible, but this does not establish that Reisen lied
  3. Because the recorder is 18 seconds slow, subtract 18 seconds to get 21:14:13; the bell precedes the light drop and supports Reisen's order
  4. The light meter times visible dimming only; with the controller reset, the action is untimed, so testimony should be ranked by the speakers' institutional roles

EX-L033. What is the strongest precise objection to the claim?12 marks

Source dossier · independent conditions
[Original] “At monkey hour, red mist crossed the lake; the gatekeeper did not see the moon.”
[Copy A] “At monkey hour red mist crossed the lake, and the gatekeeper did not see the moon.”
[Copy B] “At monkey hour, red mist crossed the lake guarding the gate; one did not see the moon.”
[Catalogue] Copy B used a kappa auto-segmenter known to join the characters around “lake; gatekeeper.”
Researcher: “B proves the red mist guarded a gate by the lake.”
  1. The claim depends on a known segmentation failure and conflicts with original punctuation plus independent Copy A; B is not independent semantic evidence
  2. The segmentation fault shows possible machine bias only; once disclosed, B can still join the original and A with equal weight in a semantic majority vote
  3. The original and Copy A make the gatekeeper the subject, but mist may have acquired guarding ability; the objection is only that ability evidence is missing
  4. B presents a different syntax that may preserve another oral version; count the original, A, and B as one vote each and seek more copies

EX-C022. Can the rule be deemed satisfied solely because the central referee saw the line? Why?10 marks

[Spell incident] A saw the red exit line; B says a lunar rainbow hid it; central referee saw it.
[Video] Central camera normal; B-side camera polarizer reversed; two of three spectators there also failed to see the line.
[Rule] Every participant must be able to identify the exit signal from their activity zone.
  1. No. The rule applies to each participant's zone; B-side observations remain, and the faulty camera weakens video without erasing testimony
  2. No. The reversed polarizer and two spectators prove the lunar rainbow was inherently dangerous on B's side, invalidating the entire contest

  4. Yes. The referee administers exit rulings, so a normal central view satisfies the institutional function; participant views affect convenience only

EX-H022. Which design comes closest to identifying the causal effect without asking faiths to abandon their practices?14 marks

Source dossier · independent conditions

  1. Pool students across faiths, stratify by weather, and compare lectures; a large sample averages away faith differences
  2. Randomize Moriya lectures on clear days, then extrapolate that clean causal effect to the temple and hermitage
  3. Add historical years and propensity scores by faith and weather, using extra-session count as a proxy for the hermitage's foreseen interest
  4. Within dates each faith deems feasible, publicly randomize this week versus next, estimate by faith, and record weather and prior interest

EX-H033. What textual mechanism most strongly explains the shift from brief control to long blockade?14 marks

Source dossier · independent conditions

  1. The draft's “seal” and three full-moon controls corroborate each other; the chronology simply calls a repeated long-lived rabbit policy long-term
