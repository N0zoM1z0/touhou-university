LU-L011. Which notice is minimally sufficient to correct both the event and the paper's self-narrative?12 marks

Source dossier
[Bunbunmaru first edition 06:40] “Mountain Gate closed all day; everyone reroute via Misty Lake.”
[Gate log 06:35] 06:30–07:10 only large freight barred; student cards admitted.
[Extra 07:05] “Gate reopened after this paper intervened.”
[Correction 11:50] “All-day closure” changed to “partial control”; extra unchanged.
[Windway log] No gate-state change around 07:05.
  1. The gate had a partial freight restriction; the first edition wrongly called it an all-day closure. No reopening occurred at 07:05, so the extra's claim that the paper caused one is also unsupported
  2. Change “everyone” to “most people”
  3. State only that students could pass; the extra need not be addressed
  4. Because the paper later corrected itself, its 07:05 reopening story becomes true
Answer: A

ExplanationBoth the exaggerated scope and the nonexistent state transition must be corrected; changing one quantifier leaves the invented causal story intact.

LU-L022. Which conclusion is most resistant to falsification from the supplied evidence?12 marks

Source dossier
[Village calendar] Mist at rooster hour on day nine; bell stopped at dog hour.
[Lunar-duty record] Same night labelled day ten; bell stopped half a watch before mist.
[Keine lecture note] Village date changes at midnight; lunar duty date changes at rooster hour.
[Belfry log] “Mist came first” was added later in newer ink.
  1. The lunar record must be forged because its date is ahead
  2. Date conventions can explain day nine versus ten, but event order still conflicts, and the later “mist first” addition is not independent evidence
  3. Newer ink means a more accurate observation
  4. Different dates prove they cannot describe the same night
Answer: B

ExplanationSeparate conventional date disagreement from the real ordering conflict. The dependent later addition cannot independently break the tie.

LU-L033. Without inventing a new clause, which reading is most rigorous?12 marks

Source dossier
[Rule v3.1] Absence means not entering the scheduled classroom.
[Academic supplement v3.1b] If a room moves by boundary, the teacher's space counts as the classroom.
[Yukari notice] Today's room is “between presence and absence”; teacher remains at the old lectern.
[Access log] Student A enters the old room; spatial label says “not scheduled classroom.”
  1. Because the teacher did not move, the room cannot have moved
  2. The spatial label alone makes A absent
  3. v3.1b supports presence because A entered the teacher's space, but whether “scheduled classroom” and “classroom” are identical remains a version gap
  4. Yukari's quotation marks invalidate all access data
Answer: C

ExplanationThe supplement provides an operational basis for presence but does not explicitly resolve the main rule's “scheduled” qualifier.

LU-H011. Which research conclusion is most defensible?14 marks

Source dossier
[A Village] Thick by midnight; writer's clock 15 min slow.
[B Mountain] Mist after the sixth eastward wind reading; vane sampled every 30 min.
[C Misty Lake] Mist 8 min after the moon reflection vanished; standard disappearance 23:42.
[D 50 years later] All Gensokyo entered mist simultaneously at midnight.
  1. D proves exact simultaneity
  2. C gives about 23:50; correcting A may place its midnight reading at 00:15. B lacks a start reading. The data do not establish Gensokyo-wide simultaneity
  3. A and C differ by ten minutes, so they must be separate incidents
  4. B is most precise because it mentions six readings
Answer: B

ExplanationNormalize sources to a common clock. B lacks an origin and D is late synthesis, so neither proves simultaneity.
