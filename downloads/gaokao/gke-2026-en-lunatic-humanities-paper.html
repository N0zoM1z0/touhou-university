LU-L011. Which notice is minimally sufficient to correct both the event and the paper's self-narrative?12 marks

Source dossier · independent conditions
[Bunbunmaru first edition 06:40] “Mountain Gate closed all day; everyone reroute via Misty Lake.”
[Gate log 06:35] 06:30–07:10 only large freight barred; student cards admitted.
[Extra 07:05] “Gate reopened after this paper intervened.”
[Correction 11:50] “All-day closure” changed to “partial control”; extra unchanged.
[Windway log] No gate-state change around 07:05.
  1. No state change occurred around 07:05, disproving the reopening story and showing that no gate restriction existed that day
  2. The gate had a partial freight restriction; the first edition wrongly called it an all-day closure. No reopening occurred at 07:05, so the extra's claim that the paper caused one is also unsupported
  3. The gate restricted large freight only; replace “everyone” with “freight.” The 07:05 extra is the paper's self-assessment of its influence, not an access instruction, so gate logs need not govern its correction
  4. Student cards remained valid, so the first edition misstated scope; despite no gate change, the paper's intervention may count as preventing a wider closure

LU-L022. Which conclusion is most resistant to falsification from the supplied evidence?12 marks

Source dossier · independent conditions

[Keine lecture note] Village date changes at midnight; lunar duty date changes at rooster hour.
[Belfry log] “Mist came first” was added later in newer ink.
  1. The newer-ink addition can be treated as later belfry verification supporting the village event order
  2. The date conventions reconcile days nine and ten, so the lunar record's bell-first order should override the village record
  3. Date conventions explain the date gap; treat the lunar “half-watch earlier” as rounding during conversion to village time, allowing both records to be combined as mist before bell
  4. Date conventions can explain day nine versus ten, but event order still conflicts, and the later “mist first” addition is not independent evidence

LU-L033. Without inventing a new clause, which reading is most rigorous?12 marks

Source dossier · independent conditions
[Rule v3.1] Absence means not entering the scheduled classroom.
[Academic supplement v3.1b] If a room moves by boundary, the teacher's space counts as the classroom.
[Yukari notice] Today's room is “between presence and absence”; teacher remains at the old lectern.
[Access log] Student A enters the old room; spatial label says “not scheduled classroom.”
  1. v3.1b supports presence because A entered the teacher's space, but whether “scheduled classroom” and “classroom” are identical remains a version gap
  2. The supplement defines teacher-space as the classroom and shares the v3.1 series with the main rule, so it should be treated as a complete operational definition of “scheduled”; A must be present
  3. Because the teacher stayed at the old lectern, no boundary movement occurred; v3.1b does not apply and the access label alone decides
  4. The main rule's “scheduled classroom” controls; because the spatial label denies that status, A is absent

LU-H011. Which research conclusion is most defensible?14 marks

Source dossier · independent conditions
[A Village] Thick by midnight; writer's clock 15 min slow.
[B Mountain] Mist after the sixth eastward wind reading; vane sampled every 30 min.
[C Misty Lake] Mist 8 min after the moon reflection vanished; standard disappearance 23:42.
[D 50 years later] All Gensokyo entered mist simultaneously at midnight.
  1. C gives about 23:50; A may correct to 00:15. B lacks an origin, so Gensokyo-wide simultaneity is not established
  2. D fixes midnight as a Gensokyo-wide reference; align A, B, and C to D before judging local deviations
  3. B's six half-watch readings place mist three hours after observation began; even without a start time, sequence length makes it more precise than A or C
  4. A corrects to 00:15 and C to about 23:50; the 25-minute gap exceeds both records' stated precision and therefore rules out one mist event

LU-H033. Which conclusion best matches these observational data?14 marks

Source dossier · independent conditions
Study claims public correction raises trust. Aya's paper: 12 corrections, trust 42→55; Hatate's: 2 corrections, 51→54. Aya simultaneously switched to free delivery and ran a popular incident map.
  1. Aya had ten more corrections and gained ten more points; after removing the shared three-point period trend, each correction adds about one trust point
  2. Aya's corrections and trust rose together, but free delivery, the popular series, and baseline differences prevent estimating an independent correction effect
  3. Both papers gained trust and the one with more corrections gained more; this supports a positive causal direction, though not its size
