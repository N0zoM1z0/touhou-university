LU-L011. Which notice is minimally sufficient to correct both the event and the paper's self-narrative?12 marks

Source dossier · independent conditions
[Bunbunmaru first edition 06:40] “Mountain Gate closed all day; everyone reroute via Misty Lake.”
[Gate log 06:35] 06:30–07:10 only large freight barred; student cards admitted.
[Extra 07:05] “Gate reopened after this paper intervened.”
[Correction 11:50] “All-day closure” changed to “partial control”; extra unchanged.
[Windway log] No gate-state change around 07:05.
  1. The gate restricted large freight only; replace “everyone” with “freight.” The 07:05 extra is the paper's self-assessment of its influence, not an access instruction, so gate logs need not govern its correction
  2. Student cards remained valid, so the first edition misstated scope; despite no gate change, the paper's intervention may count as preventing a wider closure
  3. No state change occurred around 07:05, disproving the reopening story and showing that no gate restriction existed that day
  4. The gate had a partial freight restriction; the first edition wrongly called it an all-day closure. No reopening occurred at 07:05, so the extra's claim that the paper caused one is also unsupported
Answer: D

ExplanationBoth the exaggerated scope and the nonexistent state transition must be corrected; changing one quantifier leaves the invented causal story intact.

LU-L022. Which conclusion is most resistant to falsification from the supplied evidence?12 marks

Source dossier · independent conditions
[Village calendar] Mist at rooster hour on day nine; bell stopped at dog hour.
[Lunar-duty record] Same night labelled day ten; bell stopped half a watch before mist.
[Keine lecture note] Village date changes at midnight; lunar duty date changes at rooster hour.
[Belfry log] “Mist came first” was added later in newer ink.
  1. Date conventions can explain day nine versus ten, but event order still conflicts, and the later “mist first” addition is not independent evidence
  2. The newer-ink addition can be treated as later belfry verification supporting the village event order
  3. The date conventions reconcile days nine and ten, so the lunar record's bell-first order should override the village record
  4. Date conventions explain the date gap; treat the lunar “half-watch earlier” as rounding during conversion to village time, allowing both records to be combined as mist before bell
Answer: A

ExplanationSeparate conventional date disagreement from the real ordering conflict. The dependent later addition cannot independently break the tie.

LU-L033. Without inventing a new clause, which reading is most rigorous?12 marks

Source dossier · independent conditions
[Rule v3.1] Absence means not entering the scheduled classroom.
[Academic supplement v3.1b] If a room moves by boundary, the teacher's space counts as the classroom.
[Yukari notice] Today's room is “between presence and absence”; teacher remains at the old lectern.
[Access log] Student A enters the old room; spatial label says “not scheduled classroom.”
  1. Because the teacher stayed at the old lectern, no boundary movement occurred; v3.1b does not apply and the access label alone decides
  2. The main rule's “scheduled classroom” controls; because the spatial label denies that status, A is absent
  3. v3.1b supports presence because A entered the teacher's space, but whether “scheduled classroom” and “classroom” are identical remains a version gap
  4. The supplement defines teacher-space as the classroom and shares the v3.1 series with the main rule, so it should be treated as a complete operational definition of “scheduled”; A must be present
Answer: C

ExplanationThe supplement provides an operational basis for presence but does not explicitly resolve the main rule's “scheduled” qualifier.

LU-S022. Which next-round design best separates drug, phase, order, and natural course?14 marks

Source dossier · independent conditions
Same 12 patients: drug A on a full-moon night, drug B next new moon; A appears faster. Everyone receives A then B, and illness naturally improves over time.
  1. Randomize A and B within both phases and randomize A→B or B→A, with washout and blinded assessment
  2. Keep A→B for everyone, move the second dose earlier, and blind assessment to reduce natural recovery and observer bias
  3. Let patients choose the drug while researchers randomize phase; adjust drug preference as a covariate in analysis
  4. Split patients equally between A at full moon and B at new moon; match baseline illness to estimate drug and phase together
Answer: A

ExplanationA balanced crossover identifies drug, phase, and order effects; washout/blinding address carryover and assessment bias.

LU-S033. Which follow-up best discriminates the claim that B's extra component causes the larger blast?14 marks

Source dossier · independent conditions
Starlight samples: A “back shelf of Kirisame shop,” B “Kourindou Outside-World crate,” C “Patchouli cabinet seven.” A/C share main peak; B has an extra peak. Same spell radii: 8.1, 11.7, 8.0. No dark calibration before B.
  1. Rescan A, B, and C on one calibrated instrument, control spell and order, and infer causation from a peak-height dose response
  2. Add B's extra component to both A and C; if both blasts grow, sufficiency and necessity are proved without testing B after removal
  3. Recalibrate and rescan; add B's extra component to A and C, test B without it, and blind and randomize casting
  4. Recalibrate and repeatedly randomize B castings; if the extra peak and larger blast recur together, infer a causal component effect
Answer: C

ExplanationRule out an instrumental peak, then use addition/removal to test necessity and sufficiency.
